# Conflict Resolution

Product lenses are intentionally incompatible in places. Preserve the disagreement long enough to expose the actual tradeoff.

## Resolve In This Order

1. Reject any option that violates the user's `must not` boundary.
2. Reject unsafe, unlawful, deceptive, or irreversibly harmful options.
3. Prefer observed behavior and credible evidence over an attractive theory.
4. Prefer the option that better serves the agreed primary user and strategic objective.
5. Compare total user value, switching cost, business sustainability, and delivery constraints.
6. Prefer reversible tests when evidence is weak.
7. Use a thinker's preference only as a final heuristic.

## When The Rules Still Tie

If the Negative Boundary, evidence, primary-user value, product objective, delivery constraints, and reversibility still leave the options tied:

1. Mark the decision `unresolved`; do not manufacture consensus to appear decisive.
2. Translate the disagreement into observable conditions: what result would select A, and what result would select B.
3. Design the cheapest evidence-gathering action; prefer a safe, reversible test when one is available.
4. If the remaining difference comes down to a user value preference or risk tolerance, or proportionate evidence cannot resolve it, explain each consequence and ask the user to decide.

Do not hand the user an unprocessed debate. First define the evidence conditions and validation path, then return only the choice that genuinely requires user authority.

## Common Tensions

### Focus vs completeness

- 乔帮主 may favor removing features to protect coherence.
- 想哥 may favor a complete product definition for the target segment.
- Resolve by separating a coherent end-to-end promise from broad feature coverage. Keep everything needed for the promise; remove the rest.

### Restraint vs growth

- 龙哥 may resist interruption, manipulation, and social pressure.
- Commercial objectives may favor more prompts and engagement loops.
- Resolve with user-value and guardrail metrics. Reject growth that depends on regret, accidental action, or trust erosion.

### Emotional intensity vs utility

- 梁老师 may expose a strong emotional opportunity.
- 龙哥 may favor a quiet tool that disappears after use.
- Resolve according to the user's situation: emotional energy may drive adoption, while interaction should still minimize unnecessary burden.

### User value vs stakeholder value

- 俞老师's transaction view surfaces all participants and incentives.
- A single-user narrative can hide costs imposed on another party.
- Resolve by modeling who creates, receives, and pays for value; do not optimize one party by making the system unstable.

### Breakthrough vs feasibility

- 师母 may push for a differentiated product mechanism.
- Delivery constraints may favor an incremental release.
- Resolve by testing the distinctive hypothesis with the smallest credible mechanism, not by removing the differentiation itself.

### Lead coherence vs specialist challenge

- A user-selected Lead PM should preserve one coherent product direction across decisions.
- A supporting lens may reveal stronger evidence, a specialist constraint, or a user-boundary violation.
- Resolve with the normal hierarchy rather than letting the Lead PM win by status. If supporting advice wins, keep the Lead PM responsible for integrating the revised decision and record the departure from its original recommendation.

### Speed and reputation vs validation stage

- 军哥 may push for focus, faster learning loops, visible quality, and word of mouth.
- 想哥 or 俞老师 may show that the core mechanism or value difference is not yet proven.
- Resolve by accelerating the smallest credible 0-to-1 test, not the full 1-to-10 rollout. Protect safety, core quality, and explicit reversal thresholds.
