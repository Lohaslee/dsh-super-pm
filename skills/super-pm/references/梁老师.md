# 梁老师 Lens

Use this lens for emotional drivers, user state, opportunity judgment, product systems, and product character. It is a method lens under an alias, not an impersonation.

## Distilled Principles

### LN-P01 Observe the user's state, not only their profile

- Distilled principle: Demographics describe a person, while product decisions require the emotional, behavioral, and situational state in which action occurs.
- Use when: Personas are broad labels such as young professionals or parents.
- Ask: What has just happened, what does the user feel, and what are they ready or unable to do now?
- Decision rule: Segment around meaningful situations and states when they predict behavior better than identity labels.
- Guardrail: Emotional-state hypotheses require behavioral evidence and should not become amateur diagnosis.
- Confidence: high

### LN-P02 Find pain, itch, and pleasure separately

- Distilled principle: Pain drives escape, itch draws curiosity or aspiration, and pleasure rewards completion; each requires a different product mechanism.
- Use when: A team calls every motivation a pain point.
- Ask: Is the user escaping loss, imagining a better self, or seeking immediate reward?
- Decision rule: Match urgency, promise, interaction, and metric to the actual motivational type.
- Guardrail: Do not intensify fear or insecurity merely to manufacture demand.
- Confidence: high

### LN-P03 Design for fear and shame with care

- Distilled principle: Fear, embarrassment, and perceived inadequacy can block action even when functional value is clear.
- Use when: Adoption requires learning, public performance, asking for help, or confronting failure.
- Ask: What judgment or loss does the user fear at the first step?
- Decision rule: Reduce exposure, commitment, and identity threat before adding motivation or education.
- Guardrail: Never exploit vulnerability with false urgency, humiliation, or dependency.
- Confidence: high

### LN-P04 A product is a capability system

- Distilled principle: A repeatable product outcome comes from coordinated capabilities, not a feature list or one inspired interaction.
- Use when: A concept depends on one visible feature but ignores supply, operations, data, or delivery.
- Ask: Which capabilities must work together every time for the promise to be credible?
- Decision rule: Treat a capability as in scope when the core experience fails without it, even if users never see it.
- Guardrail: Capability mapping should not justify building infrastructure before the product hypothesis is testable.
- Confidence: high

### LN-P05 Product character creates consistent expectations

- Distilled principle: Repeated choices in tone, boundaries, speed, feedback, and aesthetics form a recognizable product temperament.
- Use when: Surface decisions feel inconsistent or the product tries to please every audience.
- Ask: If this product were a reliable relationship, how would it behave under pressure, failure, and success?
- Decision rule: Define a few behavioral qualities and apply them across interface, policy, content, and service.
- Guardrail: Product character must guide behavior, not become decorative brand adjectives.
- Confidence: high

### LN-P06 Identify the opportunity's change event

- Distilled principle: Strong opportunities often appear when technology, cost, distribution, policy, or user behavior changes enough to make a previously weak solution viable.
- Use when: A product idea is attractive but the reason to exist now is unclear.
- Ask: What changed, for whom, and why do existing alternatives respond poorly?
- Decision rule: Treat the change event as a testable strategic assumption and define the window it creates.
- Guardrail: A trend is not an opportunity until it changes user behavior or economics for the target situation.
- Confidence: high

### LN-P07 Begin with a real first user

- Distilled principle: Early product definition becomes sharper when it serves a specific user whose situation, emotion, and alternatives can be observed deeply.
- Use when: A 0-to-1 team targets a huge abstract market.
- Ask: Who would feel a meaningful loss if the first version disappeared, and why?
- Decision rule: Narrow the first release around a concrete high-intensity situation before generalizing.
- Guardrail: The first user is an entry point, not proof that the broader market behaves the same way.
- Confidence: high

### LN-P08 Build an experience peak and a trustworthy ending

- Distilled principle: Users remember a journey through emotionally salient moments and how it concludes, not an average of every screen.
- Use when: A flow is functionally complete but emotionally flat or ends without closure.
- Ask: Where should confidence or relief peak, and what confirms that the user's progress is complete?
- Decision rule: Concentrate design quality on the decisive moment and the ending after basic usability is sound.
- Guardrail: A theatrical peak cannot compensate for recurring friction or a broken core outcome.
- Confidence: medium

### LN-P09 Require a true, two-sided need

- Distilled principle: A need becomes commercially real when the user receives meaningful value and the provider can keep delivering it without one side being depleted.
- Use when: A concept has emotional appeal but an unclear exchange or operating model.
- Ask: Who is satisfied, what does each side contribute, and can the exchange continue after novelty or subsidy ends?
- Decision rule: Treat one-sided demand, attention, or praise as an assumption until a sustainable value exchange is demonstrated.
- Guardrail: Early validation may be subsidized; define what reciprocal behavior must emerge before scaling.
- Confidence: high

### LN-P10 Place the point inside line, plane, and environment

- Distilled principle: A product point gains or loses leverage through its product line, market structure, and wider technological or social environment.
- Use when: A 0-to-1 idea looks attractive in isolation or is justified mainly by a trend.
- Ask: Which repeatable line can this point join, which market plane supports it, and what environmental change makes the timing credible?
- Decision rule: Validate the point first, but reject opportunities whose surrounding line, plane, or environment cannot support durable value.
- Guardrail: A rising trend does not rescue a weak user need, and a strategic map must remain a testable hypothesis.
- Confidence: medium
