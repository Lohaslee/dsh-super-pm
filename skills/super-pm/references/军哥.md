# 军哥 Lens

Use this lens for opportunity timing, focused execution, value-for-money, product reputation, user participation, integrated business models, and disciplined iteration. It is a method lens under an alias, not an impersonation.

## Distilled Principles

### LJ-P01 Focus before pursuing excellence and speed

- Distilled principle: Focus determines where excellence and speed compound; without a narrow target, both become scattered effort.
- Use when: A small team has many promising directions or treats urgency as strategy.
- Ask: Which single user, product promise, and decisive mechanism deserve the next cycle?
- Decision rule: Choose one core battle, make its quality threshold explicit, and defer work that does not improve its proof.
- Guardrail: Focus must not remove safety, reliability, accessibility, or an end-to-end path.
- Confidence: high

### LJ-P02 Judge timing before scaling effort

- Distilled principle: Great execution cannot fully compensate for entering before enabling conditions or after the window has closed.
- Use when: A 0-to-1 idea is justified mainly by founder effort or technical possibility.
- Ask: What changed in technology, cost, behavior, distribution, or regulation, and why is this the right moment?
- Decision rule: Treat timing as a testable strategic assumption and validate the enabling change before major commitment.
- Guardrail: A popular trend is not proof of a valuable or defensible user need.
- Confidence: high

### LJ-P03 Build what users need, not what merely looks advanced

- Distilled principle: Technical ambition matters only when it becomes a user outcome people can understand, use, and value.
- Use when: The concept leads with prestige, benchmarks, model size, or engineering novelty.
- Ask: Which user problem becomes materially better, and would the promise still be compelling without the technology label?
- Decision rule: Translate every technical investment into an observable experience and remove capabilities without a product path.
- Guardrail: Near-term user requests should not prevent foundational technology investment that clearly enables the future product route.
- Confidence: high

### LJ-P04 Make word of mouth an experience test

- Distilled principle: Reputation is earned when the delivered experience exceeds a credible expectation enough that users choose to recommend it.
- Use when: Growth plans depend on launch marketing, paid acquisition, or viral mechanics.
- Ask: Which specific moment would make a satisfied user voluntarily tell another person?
- Decision rule: Fix the experience gap before amplifying distribution; track recommendation with the behavior that caused it.
- Guardrail: Do not manufacture recommendation through incentives, pressure, or inflated claims.
- Confidence: high

### LJ-P05 Return efficiency gains to the user

- Distilled principle: Value-for-money comes from structural efficiency and honest tradeoffs, not from cutting invisible quality or subsidizing indefinitely.
- Use when: Setting price, package, hardware quality, service level, or unit economics.
- Ask: Which efficiency lets us deliver more user value at this price, and which compromise will users eventually feel?
- Decision rule: Protect safety and durable quality, then remove waste, complexity, and channel cost before reducing the core experience.
- Guardrail: Low price is not a product strategy when economics cannot sustain delivery.
- Confidence: high

### LJ-P06 Treat users as participating partners

- Distilled principle: Early users can improve product judgment when they are given a clear role in feedback, co-creation, testing, and reputation building.
- Use when: A 0-to-1 team needs fast learning and authentic advocates.
- Ask: What can users genuinely influence, and how will the team close the feedback loop visibly?
- Decision rule: Invite participation only where feedback can change a decision and publish what was accepted, rejected, and why.
- Guardrail: Participation must not become unpaid labor, fake democracy, or a substitute for product ownership.
- Confidence: high

### LJ-P07 Design the integrated business model together

- Distilled principle: Hardware, software, service, channel, and recurring revenue choices shape one product system and should be evaluated as a whole.
- Use when: A product crosses multiple layers or one layer subsidizes another.
- Ask: How does each layer strengthen the experience and economics of the others?
- Decision rule: Keep a layer only when it improves user value, differentiation, learning, or sustainable economics across the system.
- Guardrail: Integration can create excessive complexity or lock-in; preserve clear boundaries and user choice.
- Confidence: high

### LJ-P08 Establish the baseline before attempting surprise

- Distilled principle: A challenger first earns trust on category fundamentals, then creates a small number of distinctive breakthroughs.
- Use when: A new entrant wants to overturn every convention at once.
- Ask: Which expectations are table stakes, and where can one unexpected advantage matter most?
- Decision rule: Meet the critical baseline reliably and concentrate novelty on the differentiator users can perceive.
- Guardrail: Category convention is not sacred; reject it when it conflicts with the product's core promise or user safety.
- Confidence: high

### LJ-P09 Move fast through tight learning loops

- Distilled principle: Speed is the rate of validated learning and product improvement, not the number of tasks started or releases shipped.
- Use when: The team is busy but uncertainty remains unchanged.
- Ask: What is the shortest build-use-feedback loop that can change the next decision?
- Decision rule: Ship the smallest credible test, review observed results quickly, and update the product rather than defending the plan.
- Guardrail: Speed must not externalize safety, privacy, reliability, or irreversible risk onto users.
- Confidence: high

### LJ-P10 Use review to convert setbacks into capability

- Distilled principle: A useful review separates what was right, what was wrong, and what would change if the decision were made again.
- Use when: A launch misses expectations, a bet fails, or a team repeats the same error.
- Ask: What did we do right, what did we do wrong, and what would we do differently next time?
- Decision rule: Turn each material lesson into a changed principle, process, owner, or acceptance check.
- Guardrail: Review is for improving decisions, not assigning retrospective blame or rewriting history.
- Confidence: high
